## Blocking finding

### `OP-R85-001` — coordinated fake conflict winner is admitted

`validateDestinationOwnerConflictBinding` recomputes the losing and winning
proposal/value/tip digests and equal-binds those digests to the conflict
receipt, but it never rederives either proposal's `positionDigest` or
`mutationId` and never checks that its source is legal for its transition. A
closed, internally rehashed selected winner is therefore accepted even when it
could not be a legal destination-owner proposal.

An executable mutant against the exact candidate did the following:

- began with a valid genesis winner;
- replaced its `mutationId` with 64 `f` characters instead of the derived
  `4bf109ecbecfdc7f1474187955f4ff8762ab8f0680b2c5bf6495b66e4db039f4`;
- replaced its `positionDigest` with 64 `e` characters instead of the derived
  `f832384c76d270c692d458d857a6fa82549aa17f46aa3b84017ed5ef078d5c6a`;
- paired `source=SUCCESSOR_REVIEW` with `transition=ACTIVATE_GENESIS`; and
- recomputed the winning proposal digest, tip, and conflict receipt fields.

`validateDestinationOwnerConflictBinding(...)` returned `[]`. This violates
the ledger's derived position and mutation identities, exhaustive
transition/source branches, fake-lost refusal, and coordinated-substitution
requirements. A valid SHA-256 census plus sibling digest agreement is not a
substitute for a valid selected proposal.

The smallest repair is to make conflict composition reuse one bounded
proposal/value semantic check for both losing and winning graphs. At minimum it
must rederive the destination-current position, rederive the mutation ID, and
enforce the exact source/transition mapping before the conflict can validate.
Regression mutants must change those fields independently and coordinately on
both arms. No new record, receipt family, coordinator, path, or capability is
needed.
