## Blocking findings

### `OP-R78-001` — use intent and genesis have an unconstructible digest fixed point

`state-mutation-bootstrap-anchor-use-intent/v1` canonically embeds
`proposedGenesisInput.genesisBootstrapInputDigest` (`Dgb`) and also repeats
`expectedAuthorityValueDigest`. Its digest includes the nested proposed input
and the complete canonical intent bytes. The existing incorporated authority
ledger simultaneously requires
`authority-history-genesis-bootstrap-input/v1` (`Dgb`) to contain and frame
`useIntentDigest`.

The required construction therefore contains the direct cycle:

```text
I   = H(F("bootstrap-anchor-use-intent/v1", ..., Dgb, ..., canonical intent bytes))
Dgb = H(F("authority-history-genesis-bootstrap-input/v1", ..., I, ..., canonical input bytes))
```

Neither value can be computed first without choosing the other's final SHA-256
digest. This is not an ordinary downstream equality or a recoverable crash
prefix; it requires a cryptographic fixed point. The nested proposed input also
contains `bootstrapGenesisCoreDigest` (`Dbg`), `genesisHistoryRecordDigest`
(`Dh`), and expected E0 `Dv`. Those digests themselves consume `Dgb` directly
or through the selected history head, adding more upstream cycles rather than
breaking the direct `I <-> Dgb` cycle.

The smallest repair is to keep the use intent upstream of `Dgb`: it may bind
only closed preimage facts independent of its own digest. Remove every
`I`-dependent `Dgb`/`Dh`/`Dv`/`Dbg` digest from the use-intent census and digest,
or replace them with one exact reviewed preimage record whose members are all
independent of `I`. After `I` exists, `Dgb`, GENESIS `Dh`, E0 `Dv`, and `Dbg`
may consume it and must be cross-checked downstream. The repaired acyclic
order and exact new census require another independent exact-head review before
implementation.

### `OP-R78-002` — anchor-consumption canonical framing names no post-receipt readback member

The anchor-consumption schema contains
`runtimePostSelectionReceiptDigest`. The exact digest prose first frames `Dgp`
after runtime `Dp/Dv/Dr/Dt`, then later requires runtime
value/proposal/tip/**post readbacks**. The public anchor/E0 digest table says the
same thing: it frames `Dgp` and separately frames a runtime post-readback
digest. The literal schema has value, proposal, and tip readback members but no
`runtimePostSelectionReceiptReadbackDigest` or equivalent fourth readback.

An implementer must therefore either frame
`runtimePostSelectionReceiptDigest` twice under two meanings, omit the stated
post readback, or invent a member. All three choices change canonical bytes or
the downstream E0 acceptance equality. The smallest repair is to choose one
literal member for `Dgp` and, if its persisted readback remains required, one
distinct literal member for that readback, then state their exact framed
positions and equality. No new pointer or receipt family is needed.

### `OP-R78-003` — destination-absence authority has no closed producer or equality

The E0 core requires `destinationAbsenceDigest`; the prose says it equal-binds
the exact destination-absence observation. Expired unused anchor retirement
also requires a fresh destination-absence proof. No ledger row identifies the
schema, digest domain, canonical path, persistence, producer, current-time
rule, or equality from either digest to `Dphys`, `Ddest`, the selected
observation/helper/custody profile, or the destination state root.

The physical observation schema cannot be assumed to fill the gap: its closed
dispositions are `ADMITTED|UNSUPPORTED|UNKNOWN`, and the ledger never states
that one of those records is the absence proof or that
`destinationAbsenceDigest == Dobs`. Treating an opaque caller-selected SHA-256
as absence would let the bootstrap candidate choose an E0/retirement premise.
The smallest repair is to name one existing closed admitted observation and
its exact absence predicate/equalities, if it is sufficient; otherwise define
one minimal externally produced absence record with exact bytes, path,
framing, freshness, and custody binding. The same decided proof must be used by
E0 and expired-unused retirement.

### `OP-R78-004` — successor independent review does not identify the exact reviewed candidate

`destination-owner-independent-review/v1.candidateDigest` is said to equal the
“canonical digest of the other review inputs excluding the review record
itself.” There is no closed candidate schema and no exact member census,
domain, ordered framing, or canonical-object construction for those “other
inputs.” It is unclear whether the candidate includes the outer schema literal,
destination, selected prior RETIRED triple, teardown archive, canonical prior
installation, canonical successor authority, or some subset and in what
composition.

Author/reviewer inequality and an externally authenticated receipt do not fix
an implementation-selected review target. The smallest repair is either one
non-persisted closed candidate record or one exact framed formula over the
already-required core fields excluding `independentReview`. The external
review receipt must authenticate that exact recomputed candidate digest, and
coordinated candidate/review substitutions must refuse before successor anchor
or owner selection.

### `OP-R78-005` — anchor retirement leaves an archive ambiguity or a digest cycle

`state-mutation-bootstrap-anchor-teardown-receipt/v1` contains and frames
`externalArchiveDigest`. The only anchor archive introduced by this ledger is
`state-mutation-bootstrap-anchor-lifecycle-archive/v1`, which in turn contains
and frames `teardownReceiptDigest`. No equality says what
`externalArchiveDigest` names.

If it names the lifecycle archive, the receipt and archive are a second digest
fixed point. If it does not, its producer, schema, path, and equality are
missing. The stated crash order also selects anchor `RETIRED` with a non-null
teardown-receipt digest before saying that it writes the lifecycle archive and
teardown receipt, leaving a crash-selected FULL_REQUIRED value that references
bytes whose durable preimage/order is not fixed.

The smallest repair is a one-way order over existing artifacts. For example,
the teardown receipt may bind only prior selected anchor/owner facts and the
decided absence/custody proof; a downstream lifecycle archive may bind that
receipt; both can be created/read back before the anchor RETIRED CAS; and the
owner teardown archive and owner RETIRED CAS can remain downstream. Whatever
order is selected must remove or identify `externalArchiveDigest`, state every
readback boundary, and prove every crash prefix without a future digest in an
upstream record.

### `OP-R78-006` — physical leaf bytes do not have one portable canonicalization algorithm

The ledger closes the base64url envelope, byte length, one-component rule,
OS/profile triples, and many alias refusals. It does not define how decoded
`leaf-bytes` become the Unicode component on which NFC/NFD, lowercase,
reserved-name, trailing-dot/space, and round-trip rules operate. Windows names
are native UTF-16 while POSIX names may contain non-UTF-8 bytes; the contract
does not say whether invalid UTF-8 or unpaired surrogates refuse, nor pin the
Unicode normalization/case mapping version or exact lowercase operation.

This matters because `canonicalPhysicalLeafBytes` enters `Dphys` while the
helper/profile/version is deliberately excluded. Two admitted helper versions
may therefore canonicalize the same physical leaf differently and move
`Dphys/Ddest`, contrary to the stable-owner requirement. The smallest repair is
to decide one exact byte-to-scalar grammar and versioned normalization/case
algorithm for each admitted OS profile, including invalid native spellings and
the complete reserved-name predicate. ISS-022 must execute cross-helper and
cross-OS vectors against those exact bytes; the helper may observe the result
but may not select the algorithm.
