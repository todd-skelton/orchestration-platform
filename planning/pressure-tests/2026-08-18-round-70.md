## Disposition

The remaining ISS-002 acceptance surface is not implementation-ready. The
decision authority fixes the trust topology and lifecycle outcomes, and the
supervisor contract supplies useful digest/path tables, but most remaining
public records have no normative literal schema ledger comparable to the
already-implemented simplified-authority, commit, packet, and dispatch
surfaces. An implementer must still choose public JSON member names, canonical
member censuses, scalar grammars, nullable versus absent branch members, nested
record shapes, closed enum spellings, and several cross-record equality rules.
Those choices change canonical bytes and therefore authority digests. Passing
tests written against one implementation-selected choice would freeze that
choice without an externally reviewed decision.

This is a planning/contract blocker, not authorization to fill gaps in code.
ISS-002 must stop before implementing the families below. Existing
authority/history/commit/evidence/packet/dispatch work remains bounded by its
prior exact-head reviews, but this record grants no merge, release, promotion,
installation, runtime mutation, or self-certification authority.

## Exact blocking findings

### 1. External physical identity, observation, destination-owner, anchor, and E0

`contract-decisions.md` decides stable `Dphys`, versioned `Dobs`, raw-`Dphys`
`Ddest`, one FULL_REQUIRED owner, anchor serialization, and the acyclic E0
order. It does not fix a literal field census for any external record.
`supervisor-contract.md` then gives schema/domain names, conceptual framed
parts, paths, and exclusions, but its external tables are digest tables rather
than JSON schema ledgers:

- `physical-destination-identity/v1` has no exact member names or closed `OS`
  and leaf-identity-kind enums. The types of the stable namespace, volume,
  filesystem, ancestor, and leaf components are inferred from digest prose,
  while canonical JSON order and the representation of physical leaf bytes
  remain unpinned.
- `physical-destination-locator-observation-receipt/v1` names digest parts but
  not its record members. The table explicitly says lock/time/validity remain
  fields only inside canonical bytes without naming them. Helper-version,
  logical/resolved locator, case/Unicode profile, custody/readback,
  disposition, validity interval, and observation-time nullability and enums
  are therefore implementer-selected.
- Owner value/proposal/tip/conflict, teardown archive, successor review core,
  its three nested canonical records, successor post-selection receipt, and
  owner mutation identity have domains and conceptual parts but no exact
  member censuses. The five lifecycle edges do not decide which branch carries
  anchor, teardown, review, archive, observation, source, or transition
  evidence; nor do they decide absent versus null fields.
- Anchor identity/value/proposal/tip/conflict, use intent and its proposed-
  genesis/installer/helper records, consumption, teardown, lifecycle archive,
  E0 core, and E0 post-selection receipt have the same defect. In particular,
  `globalBootstrapIdentity` is described as an “exact raw32 projection” over a
  conceptual list, but the projection's literal schema is absent. The large
  consumption digest row does not select JSON names for its many readbacks and
  ACTIVE/CONSUMED triples.
- Expiry, race, retirement, successor, exact-reinstall, and downstream E0
  equalities are lifecycle prose. There is no exhaustive branch matrix that
  maps every named field to required, null, or absent and then equal-binds it
  to the upstream selected/read-back record.

The current `external.ts` implements only `Ddest` and path constructors, while
`external.test.ts` tests only those choices. That limited code is consistent
with the decided portion; it is not evidence that the remaining schemas are
decided.

### 2. Release, cleanup gate, recovery fence, and cleanup archive

The decision authority fixes the sole active-release pointer and the
stable-predecessor activation trust split. The supervisor contract fixes ten
cleanup cells, twelve non-self edges, one fence edge, selected-tip paths, and
FULL_REQUIRED retention. It does not fix the public records:

- `active-release/v1` has no exact JSON census. Current tests use
  `{releaseDigest, schemaVersion}`, an implementation-selected two-field value,
  even though the architecture says the selected family record binds reviewed
  installed-byte proof and release identity. No decision states whether those
  facts are nested, direct, digest-only, nullable, or branch-specific.
- `activation-cleanup-gate-root/v1`,
  `activation-cleanup-gate-head/v1`,
  `activation-recovery-fence-root/v1`,
  `activation-recovery-fence-head/v1`, and
  `activation-cleanup-archive-head/v1` have no literal ledgers, digest domains,
  or ordered framed formulas. Transaction, root/head identity, release,
  gate/fence publication, selected authority epoch, prior head, cleanup proof,
  and archive bindings are described but not assigned exact record members.
- `definitions.ts` currently chooses cleanup-head members
  `schemaVersion/ordinal/priorHeadDigest/lifecycle/publication/rootDigest` and
  fence-head members
  `schemaVersion/ordinal/priorHeadDigest/state/rootDigest`, then links them with
  untagged `canonicalDigest`. Those choices are not authorized by a decision
  ledger. The ten-cell/twelve-edge and one-edge validators are sound evidence
  for the lifecycle graph only; they cannot certify the record contract.

### 3. Recovery authorization, reservation, descriptor, launch, and attempt log

The ordered native-consume/select/post-receipt and removal/revoke sequence,
authorization states, reservation lifecycle, LIVE-only descriptor,
attachment timing, and append-only attempt-log semantics are decided. Exact
public representations are not:

- `recovery-authorization-core/v1` calls its conceptual common and
  BOOTSTRAP/SUCCESSOR lists an “exact field census,” but supplies no JSON member
  names, canonical order, types, nullability, or branch-absence table.
  Authorization state, native consume/removal receipts, post-selection
  consume/revoke receipts, attachment, and both archives likewise lack literal
  ledgers and exhaustive equality matrices.
- `recovery-attempt-reservation/v1`,
  `recovery-attempt-descriptor/v1`, `activation-recovery-launch/v1`, and both
  `attempt-log/v1` arms have no exact member censuses. The prose does not decide
  names or closed enums for process/start identity, attachment, terminal
  lineage, exit/absence, channel denial, revocation, idempotency, failure, and
  recovery facts; it does not state which IN_PROGRESS fields are forbidden
  from TERMINAL or vice versa.
- Attempt-log digest framing fixes only genesis/later tags, predecessor digest,
  ordinal, and canonical record bytes. Canonical bytes remain
  implementation-selected until both arms have literal ledgers. Reservation
  predecessor-key framing and cross-binding prose do not replace those
  ledgers.
- `recovery.ts` currently supplies only paths and `definitions.ts` only simple
  lifecycle predicates. The tests therefore cannot exercise ISS-002 acceptance
  criteria 6–7 for closed schemas, ordered receipts, descriptor attachment,
  predecessor roles, full-chain verification, or folded TERMINAL facts.

### 4. The just-landed packet parser exposes a definite identity-binding defect

The packet member census, paired authority/history nullability, exact eleven
slots, outcome-to-slot mapping, complete history binding, and branch-local
`Dcommit` tags agree with the reviewed contract. No new field-census conflict
was found in `packet.ts`. The final composition does, however, expose a
definite semantic inconsistency already present at its inputs:

- `parsePointerEvidencePacket` recomputes `Dcommit` but discards the result. It
  compares the global authority `Dp` to the commit's copied old-authority `Dp`
  and compares the target slot's canonical bytes to the slot embedded in the
  commit, but it never rederives the target `Dp` from the checkpoint's exact
  kind/path/installation/project/state/transaction/source tuple and never
  rederives the run ID from `G`, target mutation, run ordinal, prior checkpoint,
  and old authority `Dp/Dt/Dv/Dr`.
- The accepted ordinary and rotation fixtures make this executable: their
  target path-instance digest is a repeated placeholder and their `runId` is
  the caller-chosen `"d".repeat(64)`; the same placeholder is copied into the
  segment/commit/slot and accepted. Equality among caller-selected copies is
  not the required deterministic identity binding. A coordinated substitution
  therefore survives the final packet parser.
- The missing run-ID input is itself a contract question for composed packet
  validation: the deterministic formula includes nullable prior-checkpoint
  digest, but the `Dcommit`/packet ledger does not say exactly where the packet
  obtains or derives that input. Implementing an inferred source would create
  another unreviewed public semantic choice.

This defect does not justify widening the packet. The smallest remedy is to
decide the exact derivation source for the prior-checkpoint input, then make the
existing commit/packet validator rederive both `Dp` and run ID from already
required evidence and add independent plus coordinated substitution mutants.
If every required input is already derivable, add no member. If one is not,
amend the ledger explicitly before changing the schema.

## Smallest bounded replan

1. Add one normative remaining-surface schema ledger to the sole decision
   authority, or explicitly delegate named ledger sections whose exact bytes
   are incorporated into that authority. For every record above, state exact
   `schemaVersion`, ascending canonical JSON member list, scalar grammar,
   nested closed record, dense-array bound, closed enum, required/null/absent
   branch matrix, persistence path, digest domain, ordered typed framed parts,
   exclusions, and every cross-record equality.
2. Organize that amendment in three dependency-ordered blocks: external
   physical/owner/anchor/E0; release/gate/fence/cleanup; recovery authorization/
   reservation/descriptor/launch/attempt-log. The blocks may be reviewed and
   implemented incrementally, but no family proceeds from conceptual digest
   prose alone.
3. Close the packet identity derivation in the same replan: name the exact
   prior-checkpoint source, prove target `Dp` and run-ID recomputation, and add
   mutants that change each identity input independently and all inputs
   coordinately while copying the forged digest through every sibling.
4. After the decision amendment, require a fresh independent exact-head review
   before implementation. Then implement parsers/canonicalizers/digests and
   schema-census, branch-absence, equality, hostile-shape, byte-golden, and
   cross-OS mutants family by family.

This remedy does not require a new pointer kind, receipt layer, migration,
proof tree, inventory, coordinator, compaction mechanism, capability, runtime
service, or consumer-specific adapter field.

## Bounded proportionality and trust analysis

| Mechanism | Concrete in-scope threat | Deletion test | Smallest credible alternative and result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Literal schema ledgers | Independent implementations choose different member names/nullability and hash different authority bytes. | Remove any census/branch row and that exact choice returns to the implementer. | Tables over already-required records are smaller than discovering a schema through code and repair rounds. Add ledgers; add no runtime machinery. |
| External owner/anchor/E0 chain | Two installation IDs can race for one physical destination or form a digest cycle. | Delete the one physical key, selected lifecycle, or downstream-receipt equality and parallel genesis or self-reference becomes possible. | Retain the decided single owner/anchor chain; specify its records rather than add another coordinator or lock family. |
| Cleanup gate/fence lifecycle | Abort and activation can both claim the same transaction, or a self-loop can fabricate progress. | Delete the ten-cell/twelve-edge or one-edge restriction and an invalid transition is admitted. | Retain the existing pure graph validators; add record ledgers, not another state machine. |
| Recovery receipt ordering and attempt chain | A copied capability, unselected native action, or forked attempt can claim recovery/start authority. | Delete native-before-select/post-after-select ordering or predecessor/ordinal equality and the exact forgery is admitted. | Closed records plus the small full chain are smaller than a summary pointer, membership proof, or compaction protocol. |
| Deterministic `Dp` and run-ID recomputation | Coordinated copies of caller-chosen digests can masquerade as the reviewed target/run. | Delete recomputation and the current accepted placeholder fixtures demonstrate the substitution. | Compare recomputed identities from existing evidence; do not add an epoch key, receipt, or packet wrapper unless the replan proves a required input is absent. |

The ledgers protect current public compatibility and authority boundaries; they
are not speculative future-consumer machinery. Conversely, adding schemas in
code now would let the worker/candidate choose the contract that later tests
use to certify it. That violates the external-review and stable-promotion
trust split. Structural parser success remains relative evidence only; runtime
authority still requires admitted custody, the selected authority, kernel
exclusion, and ISS-004's private live capability.
