# ISS-022 lock-row post-repair review — round 286

## Disposition

`BLOCK_REPLAN`.

Round 285's descriptor oracle and timeout blockers are materially repaired, but
the claimed no-kill closure is not. This is the permitted follow-up pressure
round; a second confirmed blocker requires replan rather than another repair
round.

## Confirmed blocker

`executePortablePrimitiveLockAttemptWithoutTimer` delegates to
`observePortablePrimitiveChild` (`executor.ts:421-438`). That shared observer
still calls `child.kill("SIGKILL")` when output exceeds 64 KiB
(`executor.ts:340-345`). The lock-specific call disables only the separate
first-line kill flag; the overflow kill remains reachable on the sole
post-death `LOCK_ATTEMPT`.

The structural test slices the wrapper and checks its shared observer only for
`setTimeout|clearTimeout` (`filesystem-executor.test.ts:95-116`), so it passes
without proving the promised no-kill execution closure. The executed source
closure reported `WrapperKillCount=0`, `ObserverKillCount=2`, and
`ObserverTimerCount=0`; one observer kill is reachable with the wrapper's
`killAfterFirstLine=false`. A killed/overflowed child reduces to UNKNOWN, so
this does not elevate stale ownership, but it contradicts the exact repaired
method and its regression proof.

## Bounded remedy and proof

The replan should preserve the shared observer but make output-overflow policy
explicit. The post-death call must select `killOnOutputLimit=false`, continue
draining while discarding excess bytes, record the overflow, and wait for
natural close or the outer hosted-job bound. Generic timed rows may retain their
current kill policy. A structural negative control must traverse the called
helper closure and fail when any timer or kill is reachable from the
lock-specific entry point, while retaining exactly one spawn and one
`LOCK_ATTEMPT`.
