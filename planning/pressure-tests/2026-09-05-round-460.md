## Method

1. The seven criteria were read from `planning/drafts/ISS-054.md:121-154` at
   this HEAD, not from Round 456's abridgement.
2. The corpus clauses were read from `docs/architecture/contract-decisions.md`
   at this HEAD, located by text rather than by Round 456's line numbers,
   because the ledger has grown; the located ranges are 7169-7190 and
   7191-7198. Each enumerated item in those two blocks was split out as its own
   census row rather than being grouped.
3. Test names were located by enumerating every `describe` and `test` call in
   the three landed test files with line numbers, then reading the body of each
   vector cited. Relation-mutant and deletion-mutant rows were located by
   enumerating the `relation:` and `gate:` labels of the committed mutant tables
   and reading the mutant inputs and expected issue arrays.
4. A clause was recorded as covered only when a landed vector exercises it in a
   way that would fail if the corresponding gate were removed. Where the
   discharging vector is a committed deletion mutant, the mutant row is named.
   Where a clause is discharged by a generalized vector rather than by the
   literal mutant the ledger names, that is recorded as a precision note in the
   census, not silently as coverage.
5. Every cited `file:line` was verified against the file at this lane's HEAD.
   Slice E changes no test file, so those lines are identical at `149d9e8`.
6. The cross-file census vectors were checked explicitly, because that is the
   coupling Packet D's first hosted run failed on: the public-surface and
   absent-export censuses in `repository-protection.test.ts` (RP:1541, RP:2488,
   RP:3795) and `repository-protection-binding.test.ts` (RPB:2213, RPB:2249,
   RPB:2269). RP:3795's absent-name list is asserted against the real
   `packages/contracts` public surface, so it is head-sensitive by
   construction; the eleven names at RPB:2234-2244 and the three at
   RP:3806-3808 are the live constraint on any later ISS-054 or ISS-038 packet.

## Review attack surface

1. **A criterion or clause marked satisfied by a test that does not exist at
   the cited line.** Every cited `file:line` should be re-derived from the three
   test files at `149d9e8` by the confirming lane, not trusted from this record.
2. **A corpus clause mapped to a tautological vector** - a vector that would
   still pass with the gate it claims to prove physically removed. The highest
   risk rows are the ones citing a broad matrix (RP:916) or a whole mutant table
   (RP:2825, RPB:1987) rather than a single assertion; the confirming lane
   should spot-check that the named mutant row, not the table's mere existence,
   discharges the clause.
3. **A gap omitted.** The census claims exactly one gap. A confirming lane
   should re-split the two ledger blocks into clauses independently and compare
   the clause list, not only the dispositions - a clause silently merged into a
   neighbour reads as coverage.
4. **A precision note used to launder a gap.** Three clauses are recorded as
   discharged by a generalized vector. If the confirming lane judges any of the
   three to be a real gap rather than a precision note, that is a legitimate
   disagreement and should be recorded as such.
5. **A closure claim smuggled into a census.** The draft record and this record
   must both read as findings, not as a decision. Any sentence that could be
   quoted as "ISS-054 is closed", "confirmed" or "accepted" is a defect.
6. **Head drift.** The cross-file public-surface vectors are head-sensitive by
   construction. If any packet lands between this census and its confirmation,
   the confirming lane must re-derive at the new head rather than at
   `149d9e8`.
