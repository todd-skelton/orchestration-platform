## Tip-only selected snapshots and constructibility

The value retains only the `Dt` needed to identify each selected snapshot:

- active release;
- cleanup gate;
- optional recovery fence;
- nullable predecessor attempt log; and
- current IN_PROGRESS or TERMINAL attempt log in later reservation branches.

This does not create an inverse-digest lookup. Each later composition has an
independently trusted way to obtain the corresponding `Dp` and then uses the
common content paths plus the named `Dt`:

- reservation transaction and source construct the cleanup-gate, recovery-
  fence, predecessor-log, and current-log identities;
- the reservation predecessor identity recomputes the exact attempt-log
  predecessor tuple/key/path relation;
- the selected current state-mutation authority and its fully walked history
  bind the current active-release `Dp/Dt/Dv/Dr`; and
- the common locator reads exact content-addressed tip/proposal/value objects,
  derives `Dv/Dr`, and validates one graph under the independently trusted
  identity.

The active-release path is static but its `Dp` includes its selecting
transaction. That transaction is not guessed from the reservation. It is
available through the selected authority/operation/history composition that
already carries the active-release full triple; the reservation's
`activeReleaseTipDigest` must equal that current trusted snapshot later. The
reservation correctly does not copy a second active-release `Dp/Dv/Dr` or a
caller-selected transaction.

Likewise, a SHA-shaped gate/fence/log tip does not prove currentness, family
semantics, source, or lineage. Later composition must parse the exact family
value, walk required history, bind trusted transaction/source, compare the
canonical current tip where required, and fail `UNKNOWN` for missing,
conflicting, stale, or malformed evidence. This structural slice makes no such
claim.

Copying five complete `Dp/Dv/Dr/Dt` bundles into the reservation would add up to
fifteen redundant digest members without adding a locator or independent
authority source. The retained tip-only shape is proportional.

## Installed definition and mutable launch-input attack

Omitting `descriptorInputsDigest`, `launchDefinitionDigest`, argv, executable,
shim, declared user, path, process ID, and process-tree digest is safe under the
existing installation contract.

The native scheduler definition binds the exact executable, immutable
installation-scoped shim, byte digests, canonical argv, installation/project/
state identities, shim ABI, and declared user. N0 writes and reads that native
definition once. Successor promotion never rewrites it; active-release
selection is the sole changing implementation choice. The reservation's
selected active-release snapshot plus trusted installation identity therefore
fixes the reviewed launch implementation without another mutable launch input.

The future LIVE descriptor owns the actual launch boundary. It must bind the
selected RESERVED graph and exact argv/process-tree/start observation, and it
must refuse moved installed definition bytes. Process adoption, zero/multiple
matches, exit/absence, and process-tree authority are downstream ISS-030
runtime facts. Adding any of them to the pre-launch reservation would either
copy immutable installation evidence or introduce facts that do not yet exist,
creating a cycle.

The earlier ISS-030 phrase that the reservation “carries the descriptor inputs”
is satisfied by the selected active release, gate/fence context, predecessor,
and attempt ID fixed here. It does not require a second digest of immutable
native bytes or future process observations.

