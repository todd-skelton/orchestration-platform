## Blocking findings

### `OP-R97-001` — lifetime global identity is copied, not derived

`validateBootstrapGenesisCoreBinding` derives the authority `Dp` correctly from
the anchor installation/project/state-root and the canonical authority path,
but it never consumes `state-mutation-global-identity/v1` or recomputes `G`.
It only compares the caller-provided `globalIdentityDigest` copies in the
successor core, proposed-genesis input, GENESIS record, selected authority
value, and `Dbg` core.

A read-only in-memory reconstruction replaced `G` in all of those siblings,
recomputed `Dsc`, the use-intent-dependent `Dgb`, GENESIS `Dh`, selected E0
`Dv`, and `Dbg`, and then called the exact public validator. It returned `[]`.
The replacement `G` was not derived from installation, project, state root,
custody instance, canonical authority path, and the already-derived `Dp`.
Hashing all copies together therefore mistakes coordinated agreement for
lifetime identity authority.

Smallest repair: add the existing closed `state-mutation-global-identity/v1`
record to the E0 composition, recompute its digest with the existing public
function, equal-bind every identity member to the anchor and derived `Dp`, and
then compare that digest to `Dsc`, `Dh`, the authority value, proposed input,
and `Dbg`. Add individual identity-member mutants plus the coordinated
all-sibling substitution. No new schema, field, digest domain, path, or
capability is required.

### `OP-R97-002` — the selected active release is disconnected from the reviewed bootstrap operation

The selected authority value's active-release `Dp/Dt/Dv/Dr` is checked only
against the four SHA-256 members of caller-supplied `coreExpectationFields`.
Those expectation bytes contain no selected active-release records or reviewed
release identity. Separately, `computeSuccessorAuthorityCoreDigest` proves that
the operation and `Dsc` agree on release manifest, installed bytes, release
subject, independent review, and installer. Nothing proves that the authority
value's selected active-release tuple is the release described by that exact
reviewed `BOOTSTRAP_INSTALL` operation.

A second in-memory reconstruction replaced all four active-release members in
the authority value and the four expectation members, recomputed E0 `Dv` and
`Dbg`, and returned `[]`. Thus even a trusted caller can report the current
active-release tuple without supplying the missing equality from that selected
graph to the operation/release/review facts.

Smallest repair: consume the existing selected active-release value/proposal/
tip and reviewed installed-release context, recompute its `Dp/Dt/Dv/Dr`, and
prove it is the exact immutable reviewed release represented by the bootstrap
operation and `Dsc`. Remove the digest-only expectation as an authority
substitute. Reuse existing pointer/release parsers and framing; do not add an
E0-specific proof, receipt, field, or registry kind.

### `OP-R97-003` — ACTIVE provenance and the successor-anchor branch are not composed

The E0 validator calls `validateBootstrapAnchorUseIntentBinding`, which
re-hashes the supplied ACTIVE graphs but does not compose the already-existing
full anchor and owner mutation validators. As a result, it checks lifecycle
text but not the initial anchor ordinal or the exact owner ordinal transition.
A coordinated reconstruction changed both selected ACTIVE ordinals from
`"0"` to `"7"`, rebuilt the anchor and owner value/proposal/tip graphs, use
intent, `Dgb`, `Dh`, `Dv`, and `Dbg`, and returned `[]`.

The same gap is larger on the successor path. The committed acceptance fixture
passes an arbitrary SHA-256 value as `Dsrc`; it supplies no successor review
core, prior selected RETIRED graph, teardown archive, or independent-review
record. Its synthetic `Dsrp` has caller-selected value/proposal/tip readback
digests. The E0 composition checks that the receipt digest and selected graph
digests agree, but does not recompute those three readbacks and only checks that
the whole-receipt readback digest is non-null SHA-256. Replacing all three
internal readback digests, recomputing the synthetic `Dsrp`, and replacing its
claimed persisted readback digest returned `[]`. The owner proposal's
`observationDigest` is also not equal-bound to the current `Dobs` used by E0.

Smallest repair: compose the existing anchor mutation validator for the
create-once ACTIVE anchor and the existing owner mutation/successor review
validators. The genesis arm supplies exact null owner predecessors and proves
ordinal zero. The successor arm supplies the actual prior RETIRED
tip/value/proposal, teardown archive, `Dsrc`, independent review, selected
successor graph, `Dsrp`, and their readback bytes. Recompute each Dsrp readback
and its own persisted readback digest, and equal-bind the owner proposal's
observation to current `Dobs`. A closed genesis/successor trusted-context union
or two branch-specific entry points is smaller than duplicating those
validators and does not authorize owner archive or `RETIRED` mutation here.

### `OP-R97-004` — observation admission is evaluated at the absence time, not E0 time

The core composition passes `Dabs.observedAt` to
`validateExternalAbsenceBinding`, so `Dobs` need only be valid when the absence
receipt was created. The separate use-intent validation checks the injected
`effectiveAt` against the intent interval but never checks `Dobs` at that same
time. The normative admission rule requires the locator observation interval
to contain the injected current UTC for the mutation.

An in-memory reconstruction shortened `Dobs.validUntil` to
`2026-08-19T12:05:00.000Z`, retained a valid `Dabs` at 12:01 and E0
`effectiveAt` at 12:10, and coordinately rebuilt the observation-dependent
absence, `Dsc`, use intent, `Dgb`, `Dh`, authority value, `Dv`, and `Dbg`. The
validator returned `[]` even though `Dobs` had expired five minutes before E0.

Smallest repair: admit one closed shared E0 effective time and require both the
use intent and locator observation to contain it, while retaining the existing
`Dabs.observedAt` ordering check. Add before/at/after observation-validity
boundaries and a coordinated stale-observation reconstruction. No new time
field belongs in `Dbg`, `Dgb`, `Dh`, `Dsc`, or the authority value.
