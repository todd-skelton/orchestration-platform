# ISS-022 lock-row pressure review — round 285

## Disposition

`BLOCK_REPLAN`.

The three-row census is closed and the focused checks are green, but two facts
needed for authority are not measured by the implemented method.

## Blocking findings

1. **`LOCK_DEFAULT_NON_INHERITANCE` is a false-positive oracle.** The lock
   descriptor is opened and retained by the `LOCK_HOLDER` worker
   (`filesystem-worker.mjs:96-106`). The process tested at `lock.ts:97-103` is
   then spawned separately by the stable parent, as the holder's sibling. That
   sibling cannot inherit a descriptor owned only by the holder, regardless of
   whether Node's default spawn policy would inherit a descriptor from its
   actual parent. Its `EEXIST` result proves only that the pathname still
   exists. Nevertheless `lock.ts:110-112` asserts
   `DEFAULT_NON_INHERITED`/`0`, and the normalizer consumes those assertions to
   emit PASS. The focused execution reproduced the PASS without any descriptor
   access observation.
2. **The post-death attempt has the prohibited timeout path.** `lock.ts:85`
   routes the sole attempt through the generic child executor, which arms a
   10-second timeout and kills the child at `executor.ts:333-336`. Mapping an
   expired timer to UNKNOWN avoids stale-owner elevation, but does not satisfy
   ISS-022's literal requirement that this acquisition have no timeout path.

## Bounded remedy and regression proof

Use a lock-specific fixture in which the stable parent itself owns the exact
open lock handle and spawns one direct child with the default descriptor set.
Record an observed attempt by that child to use the known descriptor number;
PASS only when access is refused, and remove the self-authored policy/count
fields as authority. Execute the post-death `O_EXCL` attempt once through a
lock-specific no-timer path; retain one provider-observed holder `close` before
that call and let only the outer hosted-job bound diagnose a hang.

Regression controls must make the non-inheritance row non-PASS when the holder
descriptor is explicitly placed in the child's `stdio` map, when descriptor
access succeeds, or when the terminal/access observation is missing. A timer,
second acquisition call, unlink, PID/age/lease input, or stale-owner branch in
the lock-specific execution closure must fail the structural test.
