## Blocking findings

### `OP-R94-001` — selected CONSUMED anchor and owner graphs are not closed

The teardown composition recomputes the selected prior anchor `Dbat/Dbav/Dbar`
and selected owner `Dot/Dov/Dor` digests, and it binds the two independently to
the private expected digest fields. It does not require the CONSUMED owner
value's non-null `anchorTipDigest`, `anchorValueDigest`, and
`anchorReceiptDigest` to equal the separately recomputed selected CONSUMED
anchor triple. It only compares the owner's base `anchorDigest`, installation,
destination, and lifecycle.

An independent in-memory reconstruction replaced all three anchor-selection
members inside the selected CONSUMED owner value, changed the owner proposal's
transition evidence to the substituted anchor tip, and recomputed `Dov`, `Dor`,
`Dot`, all three teardown-receipt owner members, and the private selected-owner
expectation. The anchor, physical destination, absence graph, archive, and
selected prior anchor triple remained unchanged. The public composition
returned `[]`.

The same boundary admits malformed CONSUMED lineage. One reconstruction changed
the selected anchor `CONSUME` proposal's complete predecessor triple to all
null, then recomputed its mutation ID, `Dbar`, `Dbat`, archive, receipt, and
private selected-anchor expectation. A second changed the selected owner
`CONSUME` proposal's complete predecessor triple to all null and recomputed its
mutation ID, `Dor`, `Dot`, receipt, and private selected-owner expectation.
Both returned `[]`. The parsers correctly reject mixed nullability, but the
composition never requires the non-genesis all-non-null branch.

This violates the exhaustive lifecycle matrices: CONSUMED must be selected from
an exact prior ACTIVE triple, and the CONSUMED owner must embed the exact
selected anchor CONSUMED triple. A selected owner graph can therefore attest a
different or nonexistent anchor lineage while authorizing teardown of the
current anchor.

Smallest bounded remedy: require the selected owner value's three anchor
members to equal the recomputed prior anchor tip/value/proposal digests, and
require both CONSUMED selecting proposals to carry all three non-null
predecessor members. Reuse the existing selection semantics where practical;
do not add a persisted record, duplicate receipt, history structure, or
capability. Add independent and coordinated mutants for each equality and both
null-predecessor cases.

### `OP-R94-002` — ACTIVE owner branch is chosen independently of the anchor

For `RETIRE_UNUSED`, the composition accepts either
`ACTIVATE_GENESIS/BOOTSTRAP_GENESIS` or
`ACTIVATE_SUCCESSOR/SUCCESSOR_REVIEW` based only on the owner proposal. It does
not require `selectedOwnerValue.successorReviewCoreDigest` to equal the
anchor's `successorReviewCoreDigest`. On the purported genesis arm it also does
not require owner ordinal `"0"`, an all-null predecessor triple, or transition
evidence equal to the anchor's bootstrap grant. The upstream use-intent
composition already enforces these exact branch equalities, so this is a
teardown-specific widening rather than an absent platform contract.

Two independent coordinated reconstructions proved the widening. The first
gave a genesis anchor a selected ACTIVE owner value with a non-null foreign
successor-review core and recomputed the complete owner selection, receipt, and
private expected digests. The second retained the genesis transition/source but
changed owner ordinal to `"7"`, made all three proposal predecessor members
non-null, changed transition evidence away from the bootstrap grant, and
recomputed the complete graph. Both public compositions returned `[]`.

This permits a malformed or cross-branch ACTIVE owner selection to authorize
`RETIRE_UNUSED`, defeating the owner/anchor branch identity that serializes one
installation per physical destination.

Smallest bounded remedy: derive the ACTIVE owner arm from the anchor's nullable
successor-review core and require the owner value to carry that exact same
core. The null/genesis arm must require ordinal `"0"`, null prior triple,
`ACTIVATE_GENESIS`, `BOOTSTRAP_GENESIS`, and the exact anchor bootstrap grant.
The non-null/successor arm must require an all-non-null prior triple,
`ACTIVATE_SUCCESSOR`, `SUCCESSOR_REVIEW`, and transition evidence equal to that
exact review core. Reuse the already-reviewed use-intent branch checks rather
than introducing another policy or authority object, and execute a mutant for
every branch member plus a coordinated branch reconstruction.

### `OP-R94-003` — same-lock absence is not equal-bound to the anchor context

The composition validates a closed `DESTINATION_STATE_ROOT_ABSENT` observation
against the physical identity, admitted locator observation, and the separate
private absence expectation. It equal-binds the derived physical destination
to the anchor. It does not require the absence graph's `stateRootDigest`,
`helperDigest`, or `custodyInstanceDigest` to equal the anchor's corresponding
state-root, helper, and custody fields.

An independent coordinated reconstruction kept the anchor and destination
unchanged, substituted another helper, custody instance, and state-root digest,
then recomputed the admitted `Dobs`, `Dabs`, lifecycle archive, teardown
receipt, private absence expectation, and private archive/receipt expectation.
The composition returned `[]` even though all three absence facts differed from
the anchor being retired.

This is not the same-lock `Dabs` required by the ledger. A proof that another
state root is absent under another helper/custody context cannot retire this
anchor, even when it shares `Dphys/Ddest`.

Smallest bounded remedy: add the three direct equalities from the validated
absence/observation graph to `anchor.stateRootDigest`, `anchor.helperDigest`,
and `anchor.custodyInstanceDigest`. Retain the existing private same-lock
expectation and process/custody proof; no lock record, timestamp authority,
opaque absence claim, or new receipt is warranted. Add independent and
coordinated three-field substitutions.

## Exact surfaces that remain correct

The two new record parsers use the exact ledger censuses and canonical member
order. The lifecycle archive admits only `ACTIVE|CONSUMED`; the teardown receipt
admits only `RETIRE_UNUSED|RETIRE_CONSUMED`. Unknown fields, malformed scalars,
and wrong/future schema literals refuse through detached closed-record
snapshots.

The archive digest correctly uses domain
`state-mutation-bootstrap-anchor-lifecycle-archive/v1` and frames anchor,
archived tip/value/receipt, archived lifecycle, destination absence, and
canonical bytes. The teardown receipt correctly uses domain
`bootstrap-anchor-teardown-receipt/v1` and frames anchor, prior anchor
tip/value/receipt, retirement transition, destination, selected owner
tip/value/receipt, teardown evidence, process/custody proof, external archive,
and canonical bytes. The teardown ID uses
`bootstrap-anchor-teardown-id/v1` over anchor, prior anchor tip, transition,
same-lock absence digest, and archive digest. Raw digest/text/canonical part
types and declaration order match the ledger.

The canonical paths remain
`state-mutation-authority-anchors/<installation>/lifecycle-archives/<prior-tip>.json`
and
`state-mutation-authority-anchors/<installation>/teardown-receipts/<teardown-id>.json`.
Both schemas are registered, exported, dispatched by `parseContract`, and
present in the current-version compatibility census. Exact current versions
remain readable while missing, legacy, malformed, unknown, and future versions
remain refused.

The lifecycle archive contains neither a teardown-receipt digest nor a future
lifecycle value. The teardown receipt contains neither a future anchor value
nor a selecting proposal/tip. The implemented archive-before-receipt order is
therefore acyclic. Both valid ACTIVE and CONSUMED fixtures use
`DESTINATION_STATE_ROOT_ABSENT`, the archive digest is recomputed into the
receipt, and an archive timestamp before the absence observation refuses.
Public malformed-input validation remained total in the committed tests and
inspection found no throw path before closed parsing.

These correct structural properties do not remove the three semantic blockers
above: hashing coordinated siblings is not a substitute for the required
cross-record equalities.
