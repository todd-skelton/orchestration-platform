## Why preserve-symlinks is safe here

The usual risk of preserving symlink spellings is substitution or loading two
aliases as distinct modules. Neither route is admitted by this execution
custody:

- execution parent/root and all three targets are regular/non-reparse at
  capture and every retained/current-path recheck;
- every object has exact volume/file ID, link count one, attributes, size,
  security, final path, content digest where applicable, and unnamed-stream
  census;
- execution root has the fixed three-name census and no package write/delete/
  security right;
- Dgrant is applied to the three retained files and root, not to a spelling or
  glob, and current pathname probes must resolve back to the retained IDs;
- the candidate URL is constructed from exact target 2, not candidate data;
- the self-contained RPC imports only `node:` builtins before importing exact
  target 2; and
- the candidate artifact is the exact stable-copied candidate input, not a
  writable package tree or dependency directory.

Any reparse bit, hardlink, alternate stream, file/root/parent substitution,
wrong final path, extra directory entry, descriptor drift, or target/source

weaken those checks and must not be used to justify deleting one.
