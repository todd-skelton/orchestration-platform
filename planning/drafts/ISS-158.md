## Why

Two recorded cycles show the loop spending attempts and launches without
ever changing what it asks for.

`m1-iss154-20260915T1004` attempt 1 (`cycle-1-complete.json`, ordinals 1-5)
recorded five consecutive dead `gpt-6-astra/high` author launches before
the sixth passed. The routing row had one author placement, so every retry
relaunched the same model at the same effort against the same upstream
failure; the reviewer seat has a fallback (ISS-149) and the author seat does
not.

Chase Sets #7844 exhausted four absolute attempts in
`m2-purchase-limit-20260915T0132` at one placement, and a fifth needed Todd's
ruling (`5681842811`, ISS-154) to change the model. Attempt 2 is the same
launch as attempt 1 with a different seed. The retry ceiling exists to buy a
different outcome; it should buy a different configuration.

Todd's routing ruling 2026-09-15 (PR #491, `docs/model-selection.md`) sets
the principle: model size is the ceiling, reasoning effort is how much of
the answer is search. Effort is cheaper than intelligence, so a retry should
think longer before it changes model, and the last rung should be the other
vendor so a provider outage cannot exhaust the row. Todd's follow-up ruling
the same day: climb on every failure. Repeating a placement that just failed
costs a full launch for the same expected outcome; climbing costs the
difference between rungs.

## Design

A routing row carries ordered placement lists instead of three fixed seats:

```json
{
  "row": 7,
  "review": 11,
  "author": [
    { "model": "gpt-6-astra", "effort": "high" },
    { "model": "gpt-6-astra", "effort": "xhigh" },
    { "model": "claude-fable-5-1", "effort": "high" }
  ],
  "reviewer": [
    { "model": "claude-opus-5", "effort": "high" },
    { "model": "gpt-5.6-sol", "effort": "high" }
  ]
}
```

- The author rung for a launch is the number of prior author launches for
  the issue in this run that did not end in PASS, clamped to the last rung.
  Refused, dead before or after work, review FAIL and attributed gate
  failure all count; the kind of failure does not matter. The first launch
  of attempt 1 is rung 1; the corrective author after a FAIL is the next
  rung; attempt 2 starts wherever attempt 1 left off. Attempt and retry
  ceilings are unchanged; the ladder only decides what each launch asks for.
- The counter lives in the attempt record beside the participant history it
  is derived from, so resume and replay reuse the recorded rung rather than
  recount.
- The `repair` field is removed. The verbatim third repair uses the current
  rung like any other launch; one launch at the top rung is cheaper than a
  rule.
- The reviewer ladder advances only on refusal, never on a verdict, exactly
  as ISS-149's fallback. Configuration requires the reviewer models to be
  disjoint from the author models in the same row, so the reviewer never
  equals the author on any rung; `routing-reviewer-not-independent` and
  `invalid-routing-fallback` keep their meaning.
- Participant records and learning notes add the rung index next to the
  placement they already carry. Older records without a rung remain history.

## Done when

- `RoutingRow` is `{row, review, author: Placement[], reviewer: Placement[]}`.
  `validateRoutingRow` rejects an empty ladder, a repeated placement within
  a ladder, and any reviewer model that appears in the author ladder.
  `validateLoopConfig` refuses the old `{author, reviewer.fallback, repair}`
  shape with `invalid-routing-row`; there is no migration of a live config.
- Queue composition, corrective author and gate correction select the author
  rung from the recorded failed-launch count; a launch records its rung
  before it starts, and resume reads it back.
- The shipped `adapters/chase-sets-routing.json` ladders, effort before
  model, other vendor last:

  | Row | Rung 1        | Rung 2        | Rung 3        | Review 11 primary, fallback | Review 12 primary, fallback |
  | --- | ------------- | ------------- | ------------- | --------------------------- | --------------------------- |
  | 2   | Luna high     | Luna xhigh    | Sonnet medium | Opus high, Sol high         | Opus medium, Sol medium     |
  | 3   | Sonnet medium | Sonnet high   | Luna high     | Sol high, Opus high         | Sol medium, Opus medium     |
  | 4   | Astra medium  | Astra high    | Fable high    | Opus high, Sol high         | Opus medium, Sol medium     |
  | 7   | Astra high    | Astra xhigh   | Fable high    | Opus high, Sol high         | Opus medium, Sol medium     |
  | 10  | Sol high      | Astra high    | Fable high    | Opus high, Sonnet high      | Opus medium, Sonnet medium  |
  | 14  | Opus medium   | Opus high     | Astra high    | Sol high, Sonnet high       | Sol medium, Sonnet medium   |
  | 15  | Opus high     | Opus max      | Astra high    | Sol high, Sonnet high       | Sol medium, Sonnet medium   |

  Rung 1 is the placement shipped in PR #491. Row 10 escalates model at rung
  2 because Sol xhigh costs three to four times the tokens for less gain than
  Astra's Terminal-Bench lead on debugging work. The self adapter ladder is
  Astra high, Astra xhigh, Fable high with Opus high then Sol high review.
- Tests cover rung advance after each failure kind, clamping at the top
  rung, the counter carrying across attempts and surviving resume, reviewer
  advance on refusal only, reviewer disjointness, config rejection of the
  old shape, and the shipped table.
  `docs/loop.md` Planning and `docs/model-selection.md` describe ladders in
  place of the fixed seats.

## Out of scope

Choosing a rung from issue text, labels or diff size (classification the
loop refuses); changing the attempt ceiling or retry budgets; spend
accounting or quotas; descending a rung after a PASS; mixing models within
one launch; migrating records or a running loop config; and any change to
the pool or launcher.
