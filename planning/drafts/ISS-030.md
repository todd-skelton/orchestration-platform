## Context and evidence

One engine cycle is not a loop. A released authority must invoke the next tick
after completion/no-work/known failure and after a host restart without creating
overlap or duplicate mutation. The cycle protocol and supervisor states are in
`docs/architecture/routine-cycle.md` and `state-machines.md`.
Exact native definitions and install/removal transactions are settled in
`docs/architecture/supervisor-contract.md`.

## Scope fence

In: supervisor plan/install/tick/inspect/uninstall, immutable installation-scoped
shim, active-release verification/launch and recovery fence, five-minute default
cadence, host-reboot plus next-user-session re-entry, one-tick
fence, cycle resume, fixed retry eligibility, and OS definitions for Windows
Task Scheduler, macOS LaunchAgent, and Linux systemd user service/timer.

Out: project scheduling policy, remote hosts, cluster failover, hidden daemon
installation, privileged system-wide service, or scheduler-held mutation
credentials.

## Decisions already made

The installed OS scheduler invokes immutable `supervisor-shim/v1` under one
declared user and state root; the shim verifies canonical active-release bytes
and launches their exact `orchestrate supervisor tick`. It holds no project
mutation credential; the tick must acquire/verify session and role capabilities.
Definitions bind immutable Node/shim digests and installation ID, never a
mutable release path. New ticks refuse while a prior
tick/cycle is live or unknown. `FAILED_KNOWN` and `COMPLETED_NO_WORK` re-enter on
the next cadence; `UNKNOWN` disables mutation ticks pending diagnosis.

## Acceptance criteria and evidence

1. Generated Task Scheduler XML, LaunchAgent plist, and systemd user unit/timer
   invoke the same exact installed command/state identity at five-minute cadence
   and after reboot when the declared user session becomes eligible. Evidence:
   normalized definition receipts
   plus native parser/install verification on each OS.
2. Two-plus-cycle fixtures cover complete, no-work, known failure, review
   rejection, release action, and restart between every cycle boundary. Evidence:
   exactly one tick/cycle/action identity per opportunity.
3. Fault injection in every supervisor/cycle state resumes the exact known tick
   or starts one next tick only after terminal proof; duplicate launch, moved
   definition, stale release, and unknown journal disable new mutation. In
   addition, each designated Windows, macOS, and Linux host performs a real
   reboot and next eligible user login/session from an empty ambient credential
   environment. The native scheduler invokes the exact installed release once,
   the broker emits availability or safe refusal, and a terminal next tick is
   observed without a manual start. Evidence: full fault matrix plus signed
   before-reboot/after-login scheduler, process, credential, and tick receipts.
4. Install/uninstall are dry-run/plan-id guarded; uninstall disables future
   starts before terminating the exact live tick and proves credential/process/
   definition absence. Evidence: transactional state and day-after receipts.
5. `@orchestration-platform/supervisor` replaces only its static CLI placeholder
   and passes the exhaustive argv/envelope matrix. Evidence: command census.
6. N0 installation writes one read-only shim/native definition. With N0 and N1
   fixture releases, the unchanged definition launches N0 before the atomic
   active-record replacement, refuses under the post-activation recovery fence,
   then launches exact N1 for one verification and the next independent
   scheduler tick. Partial/moved/incompatible/stale-generation records, shim/
   Node tamper, pre-activation crash, and post-activation crash follow the
   published rollback/forward-recovery rules. Evidence: three-OS handoff/fault
   matrix and native definition/process read-back.
7. The shim owns the canonical recovery-launch current pointer and create-once
   transaction/source-token/generation/ordinal transition records using only
   the schema's exhaustive portable source-token mapping. It permits one live
   attempt, CAS-advances the pointer only from a proven retryable terminal
   attempt, preserves the full immutable chain and exact fence/transaction/
   cycle/predecessor/argv bindings, and blocks on unknown process state. Crash each sequential recovery attempt
   after reconnect and at every step 13–15 boundary; later scheduled invocations
   eventually complete without concurrent or stale channels. Evidence: three-OS
   adjacent-generation, process-tree, broker-channel, and terminal-read-back
   matrix. A `cleanup-gate-pre-fence/v1` chain never changes source or attaches
   recovery authority. After it consumes, it CASes the gate to `PUBLISHING`,
   creates/read-backs the exact fence, and CASes `PUBLISHED`; it then
   writes `TERMINAL_HANDOFF`, exits, archives its source-specific chain, and
   CAS-removes the pointer; only then may the shim start fresh
   `recovery-fence/v1` generation zero. Death after publication but before the
   handoff receipt is terminalized only from exact gate/fence publication plus
   unambiguous child absence. Evidence: kill at every consume/publication/
   `PUBLISHING`/fence-create/`PUBLISHED`/handoff/exit/archive/pointer-removal/
   fresh-READY boundary, including record-
   path collision and cross-source mutation canaries on all three OSes. Before
   the first pre-fence READY, exact original promotion-owner absence and current-
   pointer absence are mandatory; a live or ambiguously observed original owner
   never races a continuation. Evidence includes live-owner, owner-exit, stale-
   identity, and ambiguous-process fixtures.
   Kill the shim before record creation, after `READY`, immediately after native
   child creation, after `LIVE`, and during terminal cleanup. Exactly one matching
   child is adopted, proven absence becomes retryable, ambiguity becomes
   `UNKNOWN`, and step 15 writes terminal receipt then immutable launch complete
   before fence clear. Exact chain archival/CAS pointer deletion permits a later
   unrelated promotion to create generation zero. Evidence includes three-plus
   failed attempts, crashes around every record-write/pointer-CAS/terminal/fence/
   archive boundary, and a second recovered promotion on each OS.
   For gate `ABORTING`, the shim supplies no child with a broker client or
   authorization attachment. Any pre-fence or pre-activation fence-backed pointer terminalizes as
   `TERMINAL_ABORTED`, archives, and is removed. The shim proves/removes only exact staged bytes and
   waits while the broker internally compare-removes pending admission and
   revokes; crashes around every staging/admission/revoke mutation resume from
   durable receipts before any fence clear or gate cleanup. Abort retains the
   observed `NOT_PUBLISHED`, `PUBLISHING`, or `PUBLISHED` discriminator;
   `PUBLISHING` with fence absent or exactly present resumes by idempotently
   completing the exact fence and `PUBLISHED` CAS before revocation.
   Activation and abort race only by CAS from gate `PENDING` to `ACTIVATING` or
   `ABORTING`. If abort wins, fence-backed READY/LIVE channels are denied and
   `TERMINAL_ABORTED` cleanup precedes revocation/fence removal. If `ACTIVATING`
   wins, abort refuses and the shim resumes forward-only active-release CAS.
   Evidence covers fresh READY, LIVE before/after attachment, both CAS winners,
   exit/archive/pointer removal, revocation, and fence clear.
   Gate/fence authority uses immutable roots plus create-once adjacent head
   histories. When a child changes one head it pauses; the shim alone appends a
   same-source `AUTHORITY_REBIND` launch transition retaining lifecycle, attempt,
   process, and roots, then CASes the launch pointer and acknowledges. Evidence
   crashes at every head-write/head-pointer/rebind-record/launch-pointer boundary
   for `PUBLISHING`, `PUBLISHED`, `ACTIVATING`, `ABORTING`, and fence
   `PREPARED`→`POST_ACTIVATION`; nonadjacent, skipped-history, root/source/
   process changes, reattach, second-child attempts, and any second authority-
   head write before launch acknowledgment refuse.
   The shim also runs the closed root/head/current initialization reducer for
   gate and fence authority: kills after root and ordinal-zero head resume only
   the exact missing suffix; malformed, mixed, multiple, and impossible prefixes
   become `UNKNOWN` on all three OSes.
8. Before launching any ordinary N0/N1 tick, the shim requires the canonical
   activation cleanup gate absent with its transaction archive verified. This
   blocks null-launch and attached handoffs after fence clear until broker-
   internal recovery authorization revocation and gate completion. Evidence:
   crashes after fence clear/before revoke, after revoke/before gate CAS, during
   archive/removal, and a no-launch uninterrupted promotion on all three OSes.
   After canonical gate removal, the shim resolves only the current
   `active-release/v1` cleanup transaction/path and canonical cleanup head,
   validates the exact archive/revocation/active-record chain, and never scans.
   Deleted/substituted/ambiguous archives, wrong head, an abort archive bound to
   another active record, and a second activation transaction all have explicit
   fixtures.
