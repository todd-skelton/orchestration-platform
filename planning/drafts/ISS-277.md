## Decision

The constraint is that an author failure caused by the host or by mechanics
gains nothing from a stronger rung, and a registry-unadmitted effort should never
launch. The simplest viable alternative, skipping attempt-level escalation and
relying on ladder refusal, still leaves environment failures paying xhigh or
cross-vendor cost and still dispatches unadmitted efforts. The chosen
option keeps every count and identity, records which counted author failures
hold the rung, and makes `resolveRole` skip a family rung whose effort the
registry does not admit, the same way it skips a null current. The accepted
downside is that a self-reported `environment-tooling` or `mechanical` class now
steers placement, so a misattributed implementation failure retries once more
on the same rung within the unchanged attempt ceiling. This choice would be
wrong if retained runs show a held-rung retry failing again with the same
`rootCause` more often than an escalated retry succeeds.

## Simplest shape

1. `failAuthor` gains a hold flag. `syncParticipant` sets it for an author
   terminal that is not `modelRefused` and whose recorded cause is `mechanical`
   or `environment-tooling`. That covers a `failed` terminal reporting either class,
   a `malformed` terminal, and a non-refused `dead` terminal; `outcome.ts:178-207`
   projects the latter two to those classes. The id is appended once to the
   optional `authorFailures.held`, and `count` and `ids` change exactly as today.
2. Rung equals `min(count - held.length, last)` in both rung sites.
3. `resolveRole` reads `models[current].admittedEfforts` for each used family
   and `continue`s past a family rung whose effort is absent there. This applies to both
   roles; no reviewer rung is unadmitted in the cited snapshot.
