## Context and evidence

Worker occupancy and safe reclamation depend on identifying the exact launched
process tree rather than a reused PID or transcript. See
`docs/architecture/reference-inventory.md` and the ownership contract from
`ISS-002`.

## Scope fence

In: native argument launch without a shell, closed stdin, startup liveness,
process identity, descendant census, bounded termination, exit observation, and
portable diagnostics.

Out: containers, remote workers, resource quotas, and orchestration scheduling.

## Decisions already made

Mechanism selection follows `docs/architecture/contract-decisions.md`; lifecycle
states follow `Worker process and ownership` in
`docs/architecture/state-machines.md`. `ISS-022` must prove the selected path.

## Acceptance criteria and evidence

1. Arguments round-trip exactly for spaces, Unicode, quotes, and metacharacters.
   Evidence: child echo fixture on all target systems.
2. PID reuse or a dead launcher with a live child cannot release ownership.
   Evidence: lifecycle simulations with negative controls.
3. Termination targets only the exact owner and descendants and returns a
   bounded result. If any exact owner/descendant remains live, it returns
   `TERMINATION_FAILED_LIVE`, retains ownership and capacity, and names retry or
   diagnosis as the next operation. Evidence: sibling-survival and stubborn-
   child integration tests.
4. Launch failures leave no ownership record claiming a live worker. Evidence:
   fault injection before and after child creation.
5. Process identity observations support resource reclaim only after the exact
   launcher and every recorded descendant are dead; PID reuse, live orphan, or
   lost identity cannot produce an absence receipt. Evidence: reboot/orphan/PID-
   reuse resource fixtures consumed by dispatch.
