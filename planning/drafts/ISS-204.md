## Decision

Quality profile: `core-library`. First locate and measure the dominant
fixture-construction and child-process operations in each of the four cases on
Windows, distinguishing synthetic repository/remote setup, native queue steps,
snapshot/proof and cleanup. Then remove only demonstrated avoidable cost in the
owning test fixture or its direct helpers, preserving private repository and
worktree isolation, native accounting, terminal hold, full assertions and
historical-byte checks. A shared fixture must still supply independent state
per case. No product optimization is justified by this evidence.

Target: each of the four cases takes at most 15000 ms in the complete qualifying
one-worker/file-serial hosted Windows test gate at the repair head. This is a
deliberate headroom target, not a claim that the small historical sample proves
a percentile or guarantees future CI: it leaves half the unchanged 30000 ms
budget for variation. Main's 13.016/13.818 seconds for the slower cases show
the target is plausible, while the repeated 16.5-17.4 seconds and the failure
show why the target requires actual measured reduction rather than assuming
the fast run is typical. If the measured removable work cannot attain it,
report the shortfall and seek a fresh scoped decision; do not weaken the gate.

Rejected shortcuts: timeout increase, skip/retry, smaller subset as proof,
serializing an already serial suite again, deleting native coverage, global
cache, shared mutable Git fixture, or attributing the failure to load/preload
without the required discriminating control. Temporary local instrumentation
may measure phases but is not shipped telemetry. Revisit the narrow fixture
scope only if before/after evidence shows the dominant cost lies elsewhere.
