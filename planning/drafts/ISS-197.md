## Why

A launch-admission ordering defect refused a rung that the provider was
serving, and spent the reserve rung instead. At `2026-09-20T16:45:52.362Z`,
run `m1-iss196-20260920T1650` item `ISS-196:1` recorded two author probe
refusals and launched rung 2. The attempt record carries
`authorFailures: {"count": 2, "ids": [".../iss-196-attempt-1/source:probe:0",
".../iss-196-attempt-1/source:probe:1"]}`, and the author intent recorded at
that instant carries `"rung": 2` with
`"placement": {"model": "claude-fable-5-1", "effort": "high"}`
(evidence `iss-196-attempt-1.attempt.json`,
`iss-196-attempt-1.author-intent.json`,
`iss-196-attempt-1.author-attempt.json`). The self ladder is Astra/high,
Astra/xhigh, Fable/high (`scripts/dogfood/routing.ts:16-27`,
`docs/loop.md:465`), so probe 0 refused Astra/high and probe 1 refused
Astra/xhigh: both measured Codex rungs were skipped and the Fable reserve was
spent, on a `type:slice` planning item, without a worker ever launching on the
measured placement.

Nothing was wrong with the models. Six minutes later, at the capture instant
`2026-09-20T16:53:19Z` (evidence `captured-at.txt`), `gpt-6-astra` and
`gpt-5.6-sol` each served a minimal `/v1/responses` request through the same
base URL and the same auth the probe uses, returning HTTP 200 with
`"status": "completed"`, `"model": "gpt-6-astra"` and `"model": "gpt-5.6-sol"`
respectively (`probe-gpt-6-astra.response.json`,
`probe-gpt-5.6-sol.response.json`). The pool supervisor's `/api/status`,
observed at `"checkedAt": "2026-09-20T16:51:40.968Z"`, reported account
`pokebashtcg@gmail.com` (`"provider": "codex"`, `"disabled": false`,
`"status": "available"`, `"routingStatus": "ready"`, weekly window
`"used": 0`) with `"gpt-6-astra": {"status": "ready"}` and
`"gpt-5.6-sol": {"status": "ready"}`, alongside `gpt-5.6-terra` and
`gpt-5.6-luna` also `ready` (`pool-api-status.json`). The three other Codex
accounts blocked every Codex model with `next_retry_after` in
2026-09-22/23/24; one non-disabled account reporting `ready` is exactly what
`docs/loop.md:697-698` says admits a launch.

The refusal came from the other probe, and it ran first. The `/v1/models`
catalog captured in the same window lists 23 entries and does not contain
`gpt-6-astra`, `gpt-5.6-sol`, `gpt-5.6-terra` or `gpt-5.6-luna`; its only
non-image OpenAI-owned entries are `gpt-5.5` and `codex-auto-review`
(`pool-v1-models.json`). `probeProvider`
(`scripts/dogfood/dispatch-adapter.ts:59-93`) throws
`QueueBlocked("provider-model-refused", model)` at
`dispatch-adapter.ts:90-91` when the catalog omits the requested model, and
`admitLaunch` (`dispatch-adapter.ts:159-181`) awaits it at
`dispatch-adapter.ts:174` **before** reaching `probePoolModel` at
`dispatch-adapter.ts:175-176`. `waitForProvider` rethrows that reason without
waiting (`dispatch-adapter.ts:49`), `flow.ts:497-503` converts it into
`authorRefused(config, "<directory>:probe:<rung>")` plus `useFallback()`
(`flow.ts:373-376`), and the rung advances. The pool-ready admission that
`docs/loop.md:697-698` promises - "Any non-disabled account reporting the
model `ready` admits the launch; a model no account mentions is left to the
models probe" - was never consulted on either refusal. The ordering, not the
pool and not the ladder, is the defect.

One documented fact is wrong today, and a second is incomplete. `docs/loop.md:695-697` says
the launch probe reads the routing status "after the models probe", which is
what makes the promised admission unreachable for exactly the case it was
written for. `docs/loop.md:692` says "The pool keeps a model listed while all
of its credentials are suspended" - the ISS-162 premise - and the capture
adds a condition it does not cover: the pool omitted from its catalog
four models it was simultaneously serving and reporting `ready`.

The cost is not one run. Every M1 and M2 launch pays it while the catalog
omission stands: both measured Codex rungs are skipped and the Fable reserve
is spent at rung 3 on work of any size, with Fable weekly at 74% at the time
of capture and row 7's measured placement being Astra/high
(`docs/loop.md:432-441`). Operator-facing observation, stated once and not
planned here: the catalog omission is a pool-side condition, the pool is
operator tooling outside this repository, and it was repaired operator-side
later the same day - which changes nothing about this issue, because the
ordering defect refuses a pool-ready model whenever the catalog and the pool
disagree, and the capture proves they can.

## Decision

Constraint: `admitLaunch` must keep one absolute wait deadline and one
reporting cadence for both probes (`dispatch-adapter.ts:32-57`,
`docs/loop.md:703-704`), must keep the authenticated `/models` request on
every admission so auth failure and provider outage are still detected
(ISS-129, `docs/loop.md:603-606`), must keep ISS-162's every-account-blocked
wait-versus-refuse rule and its diagnostics byte-identical
(`dispatch-adapter.ts:143-152`), and must leave ladders, rungs, ceilings,
`providerOutageCeilingMs` and reviewer placement rules untouched - while a
model the pool reports `ready` at a non-disabled account stops being refused
because the catalog omits it. Simplest viable alternative: delete the catalog
membership check from `probeProvider` entirely and let the pool status be the
only admission authority, rejected because without `CODEX_POOL_STATUS_URL`
there would then be no refusal path at all, because ISS-149 and
`docs/loop.md:454-456` depend on a models probe omission advancing a reviewer
ladder for models the pool genuinely does not know, and because a mistyped
routing placement would then launch a worker that dies at dispatch instead of
refusing before it. Chosen: when `CODEX_POOL_STATUS_URL` is set, `admitLaunch`
reads the pool verdict first and then runs the authenticated models probe on
every admission, passing it the model on every admission together with a flag
that suppresses only the catalog membership comparison at
`dispatch-adapter.ts:90-91` when the pool reported that model `ready`, so the
malformed-body validation at `dispatch-adapter.ts:82-89` still runs - so a
pool-ready model is admitted without a catalog check while auth and outage
detection are unchanged, a model no account mentions still falls to today's
catalog rule, and every-account-blocked still takes ISS-162's unchanged path.
Accepted downside: with the pool read first, a
failing or malformed `/api/status` now defers the `/models` request within the
same wait loop, so during a simultaneous pool-status outage and auth failure
the first `waiting-provider` diagnostic names the status probe rather than the
auth command - both still wait under the same unchanged ceiling and both still
surface, but the operator sees them in the other order. What would show this
wrong: a recorded admission that reaches a worker launch on a model the pool
reported `ready` and the provider then refuses at dispatch with
`model_not_found` - which would mean the catalog, not the pool, is the
authority on what the provider will serve, and the pool-ready signal is not
sufficient to admit.

## Don't-rebuild pointers

- ISS-162 (`planning/drafts/ISS-162.md`, `dispatch-adapter.ts:95-153`) already
  built the pool status probe, its validation, and the every-account-blocked
  wait/refuse rule against the one absolute deadline. All of it is reused
  verbatim. This issue changes only *when* it is consulted relative to the
  catalog check, and surfaces one verdict distinction (`ready` versus
  unmentioned) that `probePoolModel` already computes internally at
  `dispatch-adapter.ts:138-141` and currently discards by returning `undefined`
  for both. Do not re-derive, re-validate or widen ISS-162's block handling.
- ISS-129 (`planning/drafts/ISS-129.md`, `docs/loop.md:603-606`) owns the
  authenticated `/models` probe, `waitForProvider`, the `waiting-provider`
  cadence and `providerOutageCeilingMs`. The probe still runs on every
  admission and the ceiling is untouched; only the model argument becomes
  conditional.
- ISS-158 (`planning/drafts/ISS-158.md`, `docs/loop.md:422-459`) owns ladders,
  rungs, the failed-launch counter and exhaustion. Nothing here changes which
  rung a failure selects, how the counter is derived, or what exhaustion does.
  This issue removes an *occasion* for advancing, not a rule about advancing.
- ISS-149 and `docs/loop.md:454-456` own reviewer ladder advance on refusal,
  before-work refusal and models probe omission. The set of triggers is
  unchanged; only which observations constitute a models probe omission
  changes, and it changes identically for both roles.
- ISS-187 (`planning/drafts/ISS-187.md`,
  `test/dogfood/queue-adapter.test.ts:89-188`) owns the
  `<stateDirectory>:probe:<rung>` refusal identity and its native accounting.
  That identity string is retained byte-identical; the diagnostic is recorded
  beside it, never in place of it.
- ISS-156 (`planning/drafts/ISS-156.md`) owns the separation of provider
  auth-process evidence from the synthetic outage clock in
  `test/dogfood/adapter.test.ts`. The new cases use the same injected-fetch
  and injected-clock seams and add no real provider, no new timing budget and
  no sleep.
- `modelRefused` (`dispatch-adapter.ts:183-187`) classifies *dispatch-time*
  refusals out of a worker trace. It is a different surface from launch
  admission and is not touched.
- Nothing in `scripts/` today consults the pool before the catalog: the only
  admission composition is `dispatch-adapter.ts:171-177` and its only caller is
  `dispatch-adapter.ts:546`. There is no second surface to reconcile.

## Done when

- **AC1 Pool-ready admits without a catalog check.** With
  `CODEX_POOL_STATUS_URL` set, `admitLaunch` reads the pool routing status
  before the catalog membership check, and a model reported `ready` by at
  least one non-disabled account is admitted even when the authenticated
  `/models` response omits it. The authenticated `/models` request still runs
  on that same admission - same URL, same `Authorization` header from the same
  auth command, same abort signal - so a failed auth command, a non-OK HTTP
  status and a malformed body still fail admission exactly as today; only the
  catalog membership comparison is skipped for that model.
- **AC2 Unmentioned and blocked are unchanged.** A model that no account
  mentions keeps today's rule: the catalog decides, and its omission is
  `provider-model-refused`. A model every enabled account blocks keeps
  ISS-162's rule unchanged - a block clearing inside the deadline waits as
  `waiting-provider`, a longer one refuses, an unknown, past or unparsable end
  waits, mention only by disabled accounts waits, and malformed or failing
  status waits - with the existing diagnostic strings unchanged character for
  character (`pool blocks <model> at every account[ until <iso>]`,
  `pool has no enabled account for <model>`, `malformed pool status response`,
  `pool status probe returned HTTP <n>`). Without `CODEX_POOL_STATUS_URL`,
  behaviour is byte-identical to today, including which requests are issued.
- **AC3 One deadline, one cadence, unchanged ceiling.** Both probes stay
  inside the single `waitForProvider` call and share the one absolute deadline
  and the ten-second `waiting-provider` cadence. No second timeout, no new
  clock, no new wait loop, and no change to
  `DEFAULT_PROVIDER_OUTAGE_CEILING_MS`, `providerOutageCeilingMs`, or the
  abort-signal budget at `dispatch-adapter.ts:44`.
- **AC4 The refusal record is attributable without a host reproduction.**
  Every launch refusal recorded through the author path retains, beside the
  existing `<stateDirectory>:probe:<rung>` identity in `attempt.json`, a
  diagnostic naming which probe refused and the evidence it refused on: for a
  catalog omission, that the models catalog omitted the model, plus a bounded
  head of the catalog ids actually returned; for a pool block, the existing
  `pool blocks ...` string. The `ids` array and the identity strings in it are
  unchanged, `count` still counts identities, and the diagnostic is recorded so
  that it cannot drift out of correspondence with the id it describes. The
  recorded diagnostic is at most 200 characters and the catalog head it carries
  is at most 10 ids, both asserted in a test. This is one additive optional field
  inside the existing `authorFailures` object in the existing `attempt.json` -
  no new artifact file, no new record, no schema version change, and records
  written without it remain readable and valid.
- **AC5 The refusal diagnostic identifies its probe.** `probeProvider`'s
  `QueueBlocked("provider-model-refused", ...)` carries a diagnostic that names
  the catalog as the refusing probe and includes the same bounded catalog head
  (at most 10 ids, at most 200 characters), in
  place of today's bare model id at `dispatch-adapter.ts:91`. The existing
  assertions that pin the bare id (`test/dogfood/adapter.test.ts:684-687`) are
  updated to the new string, and that case's `/api/status` fixture at
  `adapter.test.ts:682` is changed from
  `blockedUntil("2026-09-16T19:10:00Z")` to a status no account
  mentions (`Response.json({ accounts: [{ routingModels: {} }] })`)
  so the catalog still decides it under the new order and the case
  keeps testing a catalog refusal; no assertion is deleted and no
  case is removed.
- **AC6 The reproducing case and its control.** Two new cases join the existing
  admission tests, both driving the real functions through the existing
  injected `fetch` and injected clock, with no real provider and no added
  sleep. Reproducing case: the `/models` response omits the model while
  `/api/status` reports it `ready` at one non-disabled account - expect
  `admitLaunch` to resolve and expect the models request to have been issued, so
  the `provider-model-refused` conversion at `flow.ts:497-503` is never entered
  and no `<stateDirectory>:probe:<rung>` identity is recorded. Control case: the `/models` response omits the
  model and no account mentions it - expect today's `provider-model-refused`
  refusal and today's ladder advance. The reproducing case uses the captured
  shape: a catalog whose OpenAI-owned non-image entries are `gpt-5.5` and
  `codex-auto-review` only, a `gpt-6-astra` request, three accounts blocking it
  with far-future `next_retry_after`, and one non-disabled account reporting it
  `ready`.
- **AC7 Existing probe coverage still passes unchanged in intent.**
  `test/dogfood/adapter.test.ts:471` ("admits a launch only when the pool has a
  ready account for the model"), `:597` ("composes the models and pool probes
  under one wait deadline") and `:700` ("uses the worker auth helper for every
  models probe and rejects HTTP errors") keep every case they assert today. Two
  assertions in `:597` encode the ordering this issue inverts and are updated

  `adapter.test.ts:654-657` (`["…/models", "…/api/status"]`) and the case at
  `adapter.test.ts:678-688`, whose comment, `/api/status` fixture at `:682`,

  `docs/loop.md` states the measured behaviour and the new order: that the pool

## Not built

| Not built | Reason |
| --- | --- |
| Removing the catalog membership check entirely | Without `CODEX_POOL_STATUS_URL` there would be no refusal path at all, and a mistyped routing placement would launch a worker that dies at dispatch instead of refusing before it |
| Making the pool status the sole admission authority when it is reachable | The catalog still has to decide for models the pool does not mention, which is the case ISS-162 deliberately left to it (`dispatch-adapter.ts:138-140`) |
| A second timeout, clock or wait loop for the reordered probes | One absolute deadline is ISS-129's and ISS-162's standing rule (`docs/loop.md:703-704`); a late observation compares against what remains, never a fresh duration |
| Changing when a ladder advances, which rung a failure selects, or the exhaustion stop | ISS-158 owns ladders; this issue removes an occasion for refusal, not a rule about refusal |
| Changing `providerOutageCeilingMs` or any timeout | The defect is order, not duration; every measured wait behaviour is retained |
| A new refusal artifact file, record or schema version | The diagnostic rides inside the `authorFailures` object already written to `attempt.json`, so no reader, migration or receipt format changes |
| Replacing the `<stateDirectory>:probe:<rung>` identity with a richer id | ISS-187's accounting and its tests pin that exact string; the diagnostic sits beside it |
| Retrying, caching or reconciling the two authorities against each other | Two disagreeing observations are the normal case this issue admits on; a reconciliation layer would add state the loop does not need and cannot resume |
| Any change to the pool, its catalog, its registry or its bridges | The pool is operator tooling outside this repository; this repository's job is to stop refusing on its omissions |
| Routing, quota or pace-based placement selection | ISS-162's out-of-scope ruling stands: the operator owns the marker and ISS-158 owns the ladders |
