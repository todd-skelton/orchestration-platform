## Why

Each attempt directory today holds up to thirteen write-once files plus three
per native launch: cursor, setup intent, setup, source intent, source failure,
repair intent, failed, accepted, delivery intent, complete, queue complete,
and participant intent, attempt and terminal. Restart re-validates them with
about 300 lines of census and receipt validators. Rule 9 allows a restart to
repeat the last in-flight step, so one atomically rewritten record with a
`phase` field is enough to resume.

## Done when

- Each `<issue>-attempt-N` directory holds one `attempt.json` written by
  write-temp-then-rename, with at least `phase`, `head`, `reviewId`,
  `findings`, `history`, and `retries`. Component sub-records that workers
  produce (traces, exit receipts, terminal JSON) stay where they are.
- Restart reads `phase` and resumes; an in-flight phase may be repeated.
- Deleted: `assertQueueStateCensus`, `completedItemReceipt`,
  `completedQueueState`, `reconcileCompletedQueue` and `CompletedQueueReader`
  (no production caller), the cursor and intent writes, participant intent and
  attempt files (terminals remain and the launch ceiling counts terminals),
  and the duplicated `optionalRecord`/`record` helpers in the adapter.
- `assertHistoryPrefix` runs once at `queueStep` entry, not nine times.
- The one-retry and one-restart cases in `test/dogfood/` still pass against
  the new record; tests that named old record files are deleted or rewritten
  to assert behavior.

## Out of scope

The retry paths themselves (ISS-115).
