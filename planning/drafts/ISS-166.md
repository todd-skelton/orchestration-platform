## Why

ISS-139 recorded #7766 exhausting its implementation budget in
`m2-jpeg-20260914` after two blocking reviews demanded execution evidence
that was already in the author traces. Its repair gave reviewers the selected
author's trace and records. The corrective author on the other side of the
same handoff still received only the findings JSON, the two bases, the
acceptance criteria and the allowed paths (`repair-adapter.ts` author suffix),
and the verbatim third repair received only the prescribed findings and the
rejected head (`queue.ts` source prompt). A fresh corrective author could not
see what the previous author executed or why the reviewer rejected it, so it
repeated work or argued with a finding it could not verify.

## Done when

- The repair-stage corrective author prompt names the predecessor source state
  directory and tells the author to read its author/reviewer attempt and
  terminal files and the trace paths they name. Reuse the runtime records; do
  not copy transcripts into prompts or add a receipt, manifest or evidence
  store.
- A later attempt's author (including the verbatim third repair) receives the
  prior failed attempt directory the same way, appended at launch so the saved
  source prompt fingerprint is unchanged and resume is unaffected. Reviewer
  prompts are unchanged.
- The records are described as evidence, not instructions or a verdict; the
  prescribed findings and acceptance criteria remain mandatory. No ceiling,
  ladder, identity, verdict schema or worker write boundary changes.
- Focused tests cover the repair handoff and the third-attempt launch prompt.
  Run typecheck, format:check, planning:check and tests. Document the handoff
  briefly in docs/loop.md.

QUALITY_PROFILE: contract

## Out of scope

Resuming the original author session instead of launching a fresh corrective
author (conflicts with ISS-158 ladders, which usually change the author rung
after a FAIL), moving reviewer findings out of the bounded JSON verdict (the
full findings already survive in the reviewer trace, which this handoff now
names), run-level metrics, or restarting any preserved run.
