## Context and evidence

The platform is not self-hosting until a stable released version orchestrates
real successor work through planning, dispatch, review, and promotion. See the
roadmap outcome and all predecessor capabilities linked in `blocked_by`.

## Scope fence

In: use installed and authorized N0 from `ISS-027`, released
`@orchestration-platform/adapter-self` from `ISS-033`, the exact credential set
from `ISS-034`, bounded N1 change through the concrete host from `ISS-021`,
independent review, promotion, restart under N1, and post-promotion verification.

Out: external consumer cutover, automatic recurring releases, and unbounded
feature work in the N+1 candidate.

## Decisions already made

The exact N1 change adds `modules/health-summary/`: a provider-neutral,
advisory-only `orchestration-health-summary/v1` projection from existing reduced
state, its manifest registration, golden tests, and package census entry. It
adds no authority field or external mutation. N0 remains the only authority
until promotion completes. Lifecycle states and next-cycle behavior are defined
in the release table; a human cannot substitute evidence.
The immutable installation-scoped supervisor shim and credential references are
reused. Stable N0 alone stages N1 active-release and broker-client admission;
neither scheduler definitions nor secrets are reprovisioned.

## Acceptance criteria and evidence

1. N discovers, plans, dispatches, reviews, and promotes the N+1 issue using
   released modules. Evidence: complete event journal joined to worker and
   promotion receipts.
2. N+1 cannot write N's lease, journal authority, or installed manifest before
   promotion. Evidence: seeded canaries and denied-write logs.
3. After restart, N+1 reads the promoted state and reaches a documented steady
   state. Evidence: post-promotion health receipt and next-cycle no-op.
4. A moved candidate and a blocking review both stop promotion. Evidence:
   executed negative-control runs.
5. N0—not N1—defines and aggregates N1's complete three-OS certification before
   review and promotion. Evidence: stable test-bundle/candidate receipts and a
   candidate-authored-aggregation refusal.
6. Starting from the cold installed N0 state and empty ambient environment, the
   released self adapter freshly observes GitHub/CI, claims the one bounded work
   record, creates/reclaims the workspace, materializes one immutable worker
   result, opens the bound revision, dispatches and reads all OS certification,
   obtains distinct review, and promotes that exact digest. Evidence: one joined
   adapter/credential/workspace/code-host/CI/review/promotion timeline plus
   external read-back and capability canaries.
7. The joined journal follows the five-cycle contract exactly: implementation
   result → distinct worker-result review/merge → landed-source assembly and
   three-OS certification → distinct `release-candidate-subject/v1` review and
   stable-N0 promotion → installed-N1 verification/work close. Evidence: one
   subject/type/digest flow census plus refusal for skipped/reordered cycles,
   review-kind reuse, moved landed tree, candidate substitution, and promotion
   before accepted exact-candidate review.
8. Installed N0 runs only its four embedded baseline modules while building and
   testing the candidate health-summary bytes. Missing, moved, unlisted,
   dynamically supplied, descriptor/emitted-digest-mutated, or pre-promotion N1
   module loads refuse. After promotion, installed N1 admits health-summary only
   when its embedded registry equals its installed release manifest. Evidence:
   module registry/load census joined to all five cycles and negative controls.
9. The N1 candidate adds exactly the health-summary entrypoint and one manifest
   row, then the unchanged `ISS-011` generator produces a five-import candidate
   registry consumed by the unchanged self-host composition source. Evidence:
   generator input/output and N0-four/N1-five bundle census; hand edits,
   alternate generator/config, or an adapter/composition edit refuse scope and
   certification.
10. N0 promotion prepares N1 broker admission, stages/verifies N1, raises the
    shim fence, atomically switches `active-release/v1`, forward-activates broker
    client generation N1/revokes N0, and clears the fence. Crash before the
    switch leaves N0 runnable and removes pending admission; crash after permits
    only the same N0 recovery transaction. Promotion records, but never starts,
    the N1 verification follow-up; N0 completes steps 14–15, clears the exact
    fence during terminalization, and its scheduler tick exits. Evidence:
    barrier matrix proving N0
    works only before, N1 only after, no secret reprovisioning, and refusal of
    replay/wrong candidate/executable/operation manifest/generation/order.
11. Only the next scheduler-authenticated shim tick launches exact N1 for the
    fifth verification follow-up cycle; only after that tick terminates does the
    following independent scheduled tick launch N1 for steady-state proof. A
    stale N0 executable, inline/direct N1 launch, moved active record, mixed
    broker generation, or recovery-fence bypass refuses. Evidence: joined shim/
    active-release/broker/process/tick transcript across both launches.
12. Crashing the predecessor recovery process more than once at each boundary
    from broker activation through cycle terminalization yields sequential
    recovery-launch generations and a fresh matching broker channel only for the
    current live attempt. No concurrent/stale attempt works; eventual recovery
    clears the fence before the separately scheduled N1 verification and steady-
    state ticks. Evidence: end-to-end multi-crash transcript at steps 13–15.
13. For both uninterrupted null-launch promotion and attached recovery, a crash
    after fence clear but before authorization revocation/gate removal leaves the
    shim refusing N1. Only broker-internal `REVOKED` plus archived/absent cleanup
    gate permits the separately scheduled verification tick. Evidence: joined
    gate/authorization/shim timeline and bypass mutants.
